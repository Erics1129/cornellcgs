# Carrot Defense: implemented gameplay

Audited 12 October 2026; extended to the 24-level campaign and then to the difficulty pass, the Super tier, the boss kits and the three boss challenges on 13 October 2026. This is the authoritative implementation record, separate from preproduction illustrations and runtime visual acceptance. [Balance evidence](balance-results.md) records the completed simulation checks and their limits.

## Sources and ownership

- [CarrotGame.cs](../../unity/CarrotDefense/Assets/Scripts/CarrotGame.cs) owns combat, enemy movement, currency, targeting, waves, statuses, boss kits, blindness, merging and outcomes.
- [CarrotData.cs](../../unity/CarrotDefense/Assets/Scripts/CarrotData.cs) contains all authored levels and tuning. The block between `// WAVES:BEGIN` and `// WAVES:END` (wave arrays, `HPScaleTable`, `GapScaleTable`, `BreatherTable`, `PacketOverlapTable`, `OpeningTable`) is written by [scripts/carrot-waves.mjs](../../scripts/carrot-waves.mjs) and never edited by hand. [game-data.json](../../public/assets/minigames/carrot/game-data.json) exposes equivalent route, pad, blocker, wave and tower definitions to the website (generated by `scripts/carrot-game-data.mjs`).
- [CarrotSimulationTests.cs](../../unity/CarrotDefense/Assets/Scripts/CarrotSimulationTests.cs) executes the shipped simulation (`Run`, plus `Quick` for the unit suites, `Baselines` and `Tune` for the retune loop and `Probe` for a single logged replay). Effects and models consume events; they never award damage or money.
- [CarrotPage.tsx](../../src/minigames/carrot/CarrotPage.tsx) and [progress.ts](../../src/minigames/carrot/progress.ts) own the visible HUD and local campaign save.

## Campaign and placement

All 24 campaign maps have two or three fixed routes, multiple entrances and one shared carrot; the three challenges share one arena map (below). The 15 × 11 logical grid uses `[x,z]`; world position is `(x−7,0,z−5)`. Routes are 27–59 units long. `game-data.json` is generated from `CarrotData.cs`; `npm run carrot:test` fails if the two drift. Routes can merge and share a tail. Ordinary enemies use the authored route at ground height; moths follow that route in flight and require an air-capable tower.

Pads remain at least 0.85 units from every logical route. Scenery occupies authored pads, must be shot to clear, and pays a one-time reward. Tapping scenery explicitly focuses available towers on it; tower purchases cannot overwrite it. The remaining open positions permit alternate coverage choices from the opening wave. Decorative plants do not alter routes or create extra legal pads.

### Waves and the difficulty pass

The next wave starts after the previous wave's enemies, reserved summons and carrot contacts resolve, followed by a breather of 5 s in chapters 0–1 (levels 1–8), 4 s in chapter 2 (9–12) and 3 s from level 13 on (`CarrotData.Breather`). Calling it early awards two coins per remaining whole second (at most 10; pass 2). Normal spawns are 0.78 s apart and swarms 0.24 s apart, both multiplied by the chapter's gap scale (`GapScale`: 1.00 / 0.95 / 0.90 / 0.85 / 0.80 / 0.78 by chapter; the boss gap stays 1.8 s); the advance between packets inside a wave is multiplied by `PacketOverlap` (0.85 on the pre-boss and boss levels from level 5, else 1), so packets of those levels overlap instead of queueing. Bosses arrive in the final wave of campaign boss levels. The wave event names the first actual spawn entrance.

The campaign's wave arrays are generated, not hand-authored: `node scripts/carrot-waves.mjs --write` derives levels 1–24 from the frozen pre-pass strings in `scripts/carrot-waves.baseline.json` (p = position in the chapter, c = chapter). Every packet count n becomes `max(n+1, round(n × C))` with C = 1.10 / 1.15 / 1.25 / 1.35 by position in chapter 0 and 1.15 / 1.20 / 1.30 / 1.40 + 0.02 × min(c, 3) afterwards; boss packets stay 1; the first packet of a kind on the level that introduces it stays verbatim (shell:3 on 3, moth:4 on 5, shaman:1 on 7, crystal:3 on 9, burrow:3 on 11). Injections add elites by position: wave ⌊W/2⌋ gains shell +(2+c) from level 5; the last two ordinary waves gain shaman +(1+⌊c/2⌋) and the last ordinary wave crystal +(2+c) on p ≥ 2 levels (from 7 and 9); wave W−3 gains burrow +(2+c) on p ≥ 1 levels from 11; boss levels add a swarm surge of +(8+2c) two waves before the boss and an escort of shell +(3+c), dash +(4+c), moth +(3+c) (from 8) and crystal +(2+c) (from 12) to the boss wave. **Opening ramp (a deviation from the plan, recorded in balance-results.md):** from level 5 on, wave 1 keeps its baseline counts and wave 2 takes half the multiplier, because no opening bought with unchanged starting gold survives a first wave that is 1.5–1.9× heavier; the pass is in full force from wave 3. Running the script without flags proves `CarrotData.cs` holds exactly the generated block; `--baseline` prints the frozen pre-pass pressure and stream arrays that `Geometry` asserts against; `--pressure` prints the per-level table.

Per-enemy HP and shield scale with level by `CarrotData.HPScale`, which now runs from ×1.05 on level 1 to ×7.55 on level 24 (old value × (1 + 0.02 (L−1) + 0.08 on boss levels + 0.05 on levels 1–2)): {1.05, 1.23, 1.35, 1.65, 1.67, 1.93, 2.18, 2.62, 2.67, 3.01, 3.36, 4.03, 4.03, 4.28, 4.54, 5.11, 5.08, 5.36, 5.64, 6.28, 6.23, 6.53, 6.84, 7.55}; the challenges anchor to 2.62 (level 8), 6.28 (level 20) and 8.68 (level 24 × 1.15). Two further factors multiply at spawn: `PressureScale(level)`, the only sanctioned post-tuning dial (1 by default; the non-1 entries are 0.80 on 9, 0.95 on 11, 19 and 23, 0.85 on 24, 26 and 27), and `OpeningScale(level, wave)`, which plays wave 1 of every level from 5 on at the pre-pass health (the table entry divided by the pass multiplier), wave 2 at the square root of that and full health from wave 3; chapter 0 has no grace. Summoned enemies have no coin reward. The pass's metric is **Pressure** = HP-weighted spawn work per stream second (ordinary enemies only, crystal counting its 70 shield; the boss is a separate spike); it rises from ×1.09 on level 1 to ×2.53 on level 24, every boss level is its chapter's maximum, and no level's stream time grows beyond ×1.32 of the old one, so levels get harder rather than longer. See balance-results.md for the table.

### Super: merge two fully grown towers

**Rules.** `{"type":"merge","id":source,"target":target}` forges a Super when both towers exist, share a kind, are both tier 4 and are distinct, while the phase is ready or playing (paused allowed). No coins move. The target keeps its id, pad, position and statuses; `target.spent += source.spent`, `target.mergedSpent = source.spent`, `target.cooldown = 0.5` (a deterministic forging pause); the source is removed with its focus and aura entries; the selection becomes the target; one `merge` event fires (`kind`, `position` = target, `to` = source, `id` = target, `target` = source, `tier` 5, `duration` 0.9) and the notice shows the Super's name for 2 s. Mixed kinds or a lower tier reject with the notice "Only two fully grown {Name}s can merge" (1.6 s); dropping a tower on itself and merging after the outcome are silent. `mergeArm {id}` arms tap-tap merging for a tier-4 tower that has a partner (`mergeArmed` in the snapshot; cleared by any select, clear or outcome). `mergeUndo` within 4 s of game time, only while the Super has not fired (`lastShot` unchanged) and its pad is still empty, restores the source with its id, pad and spend and returns the target to tier 4 (`mergeUndo` event, tier 4). Selling a Super refunds `floor(spent × 0.7)` of the combined spend, so merge-then-sell equals sell-both. A Super gains no range (`range[4] == range[3]`), so the merge is a coverage decision; its trait-inclusive throughput is 0.81–0.97× two tier-4s on the same pad (measured in `SuperBudget`, bound 0.75–1.08×). Boss-interacting traits (frost boss cap, gravity boss pull, book boss slow, prism refraction) are unchanged by the Super. Uproot demotes a Super to tier 4 instead of destroying it (below).

## Enemies and boss encounters

Eight normal families have different gameplay: `nibble` is the baseline; `dash` is fast; `shell` reduces kinetic damage by 40%; `swarm` arrives in dense fragile groups; `moth` flies; `shaman` telegraphs an 8%-max-health heal for nearby ordinary allies; `burrow` periodically hides for 1.8s after a 0.7s warning; `crystal` has a breakable shield and accelerates after its break. Summoned enemies have no coin reward.

All living, nonhidden ordinary monsters in the viewport provide an HP bar; the snapshot carries every hurt or shielded monster plus the 24 nearest the carrot. Boss HP, shield, actual phase and cast countdown use authoritative values. Boss phase thresholds differ; the HUD must use each boss's actual thresholds.

Bosses start from `CarrotData.BossBaseHp` (Brambleback 2,600; Lantern Regent 5,000; Crown of Winter 8,200; Old Hollowhead 10,500; Giant Tornado 4,500; Zombie 9,000; Unknown Eyeball 16,000; Portal 31,000; Asuna 20,000) multiplied by `BossScale(level, boss) = HPScale(level) / HPScale(home)` past level 12 with home levels 4, 8, 12, 20, 25, 26, 27, so Brambleback II on Cider Gate carries 8,055 HP and Old Hollowhead II on The Last Light 12,627. Every boss kit is one explicit branch of `ScheduleBoss` in priority order; an unregistered boss id logs an error and casts nothing. Random picks (uproot targets) come from a xorshift stream seeded by the level id, so a run replays identically from the same commands (`Determinism`).

**Blindness rule (one implementation, three tints).** A blind commit sets `blindUntil`, `blindTint` (0 dark, 1 white), `blindAlpha` and `blindRange`; while `elapsed < blindUntil` `TickTower` multiplies the range of every tower that is not lit by `blindRange`. A tower is lit when it is a Prism, when it has a live Sun Blossom aura, or when its pad is one of `CarrotData.LitPads(level)` (the arena's lantern pads 4–7). Nothing is locked: buying, upgrading, selling, selecting and merging keep working in the dark. The caster's `activeUntil` is only +0.6 s so it keeps casting while the screen is dark, and `SetOutcome` clears `blindUntil` so no level ends in the dark. The snapshot carries `blind` (seconds left), `blindTint`, `blindAlpha` (0 when inactive) and `elapsed`.

**Uproot rule ("destroy a random plant", one commit path).** Candidates are towers built at least 1 s ago that have not been hit this fight; a boss needs at least two towers in play, so a single tower is never targeted. The pool is every candidate within 4.5 u of the boss (so the telegraph is on screen), else the single nearest; the pick is deterministic per level. The telegraph carries `to` = the tower's position and `target` = its id; selling during the windup cancels the hit at the 70% sell price. At the commit each surviving target is either demoted (a Super drops to tier 4, loses `mergedSpent` from `spent` and refunds 55% of it; the event carries tier 5) or removed with a refund of 60% of `spent` on level 4 and 55% elsewhere (focus, aura and selection cleaned up); only the Zombie leaves a grave blocker. Owned blockers (`ownerEnemy > 0`) clear when the owner dies; root knots additionally follow the boss and expire with its cast; `cleared` and `blockersRemaining` ignore every owned blocker.

Boss casts serialize. Root, breath, gaze and uproot choose and lock their target IDs when the windup begins. Every affected tower receives a warning and committed target event; selling it before commitment prevents that target's effect. Primary `telegraph`/`ability` events drive the boss source effect/audio once (one trailing `ability` per cast, carrying `value`: refunded coins, tithe amount, dark seconds or stun seconds), and `telegraphTarget`/`abilityTarget` cover additional targets without repeating those cues. Boss death and contact cancel uncommitted casts and release reserved summon counts. Spawned adds must still resolve before victory.

## Challenges: three boss fights on one arena

Levels 25–27 (`CarrotData.IsChallenge`, `ChallengeDifficulty` easy / hard / insane, biome `arena`, tier 3 economy) share one map: carrot at (7,5); two mirrored 31-unit orthogonal routes entering the west and east gates and reaching the carrot from south and north; 20 pads, of which pads 10 and 11 (stumps), 14 and 15 (crystals) and 18 and 19 (hedges) are scenery; lantern pads 4–7 ((4,5), (10,5), (4,7), (10,3)) keep full sight during Blindness. All ten towers are open. Boss HP is the base table (BossScale 1 on a boss's home level). Waves are authored in `scripts/carrot-waves.mjs` (the Eyeball's waves 2–11 carry a ×0.9 count scale recorded in balance-results.md): 25 (6 waves, HPScale 2.62, gap 0.95, breather 5) brings the Tornado in wave 4; 26 (9 waves, 6.28, 0.85, 4) the Zombie in wave 6; 27 (11 waves, 8.68, 0.80, 3) the Eyeball in wave 7. Opening coins are 520 / 1,500 / 2,000.

**Wave gate.** On challenge levels only, `CurrentWaveResolved` ignores living bosses while `wave < waves` (a `waveBoss[]` counter), boss summons belong to the wave current when they appear, and reserved summons gate only the final wave; the final-wave rule still needs the boss and every add dead. The boss therefore arrives mid-level and the remaining waves spawn as adds while it walks (104–123 s at 0.26 / 0.24 / 0.22), so a BossFight card is a boss fight. Outcome notices read "Guardian defeated" / "The guardian wins".

## Economy, outcomes and progress

Levels 1–12 start with 240 / 300 / 360 coins by biome; the harvest road starts with a growing fund of 500–890 coins (500 + 30 per level past 13, plus 0 / 20 / 40 / 60 for garden / marsh / frost / orchard), because a tier-1 opening against ×4+ enemies on shorter tails cannot otherwise survive wave 1; the challenges open with 520 / 1,500 / 2,000 so a boss-ready roster stands before the boss walks in. Kills, one-time scenery rewards, wave stipends (15 / 20 / 25 / 30 by biome tier on L1-L8 and the challenges, 10 / 14 / 18 / 22 on L9-L24), the small early-call bonus and uproot refunds are the only new currency sources; Tithe is the only sink besides purchases. Scenery health scales ×1 / ×1.5 / ×2 / ×2.25 by biome tier. Selling returns `floor(totalSpent × 0.70)`; buying, selling and merging cannot create money. Purchases reject locked families, insufficient funds, invalid pads and occupied pads. There are exactly three paid upgrades; the Super is free and merge-only.

The carrot begins at 10 HP. An ordinary arrival costs one HP, a boss arrival three. The 0.65s bite animation applies its single deduction near the middle. The encounter resolves after the contact finishes; a fatal last bite takes precedence over final-wave victory. A surviving carrot may win after a boss reaches it, with the corresponding three-HP loss. Enemies that bite pay no kill bounty.

Progress uses scheduled enemy resolution plus fractional boss HP, weighting a boss as eight ordinary enemies. It remains below 100% until all waves, living enemies, reserved adds and contacts have resolved. A summon does not inflate the progress denominator, but it still blocks victory. On a win progress becomes exactly 1; loss never grants completion.

The simulation runs at 30 fixed steps per game second. Pause freezes movement, cooldowns, statuses, projectiles and progress. The 2× control scales game time; leaving the page/focus suspends simulation. No idle gap grants free catch-up attacks. Purchases remain available while planning or paused. The per-tick enemy walk uses a reusable scratch list; no `ToArray` copies remain in the tick, burn, mortar, blossom, gravity or frost paths.

### Fairness contract

Merging is optional through Last Harvest (level 12): the campaign bots with merging switched off must still win the boss levels 4, 8 and 12 (they also won 16, 20 and 24 in the recorded run). From level 13 on, and in the Hard and Insane challenges, merging is expected play. The Pressure dial is the only sanctioned per-level knob (pass 2: fitted within [0.65, 1.60]); every entry is listed in balance-results.md. A legal solution always exists: on every level and every one of 20 seed variants at least one expert policy wins, and both canonical policies win all 57 runs.

## Browser contract and save boundary

Commands are `load` (optional `mode`), `start`, `next`, `preview`, `buy`, `upgrade`, `sell`, `pause` (a toggle), `setPaused` (`value` 1|0, idempotent in ready and playing), `speed`, `select`, `mute`, `clear`, plus `merge` (`id` = source, `target`), `mergeArm` (`id`) and `mergeUndo`; development and editor builds also accept `debugCoins` (`value`), which the release build ignores. `clear` deselects; it does not destroy scenery. `preview` changes the displayed base tower range. The scene raycast selects real pad/entity IDs; selections publish normalized viewport coordinates with top-origin y.

Snapshots include live coins, health, waves, phase, progress, boss/monster HP, selection, `towerCount`, `cleared`, `blockersRemaining` and `muted`, and now `elapsed`, `blind`, `blindTint`, `blindAlpha`, `superPairs` (same-kind tier-4 pairs on the board), `mergeUndo` (seconds left in the undo window) and `mergeArmed`; the selection adds `partners`, `superName` and `spent`. **`cleared` is the number of destroyed authored blockers**, excluding boss-owned root knots and graves; it is not a boolean. Clearing all scenery is `blockersRemaining === 0`. New event kinds: `merge`, `mergeUndo`, and the ability/telegraph kinds `blind`, `whiteout`, `dusk`, `uproot`, `tithe`, `gust`, `liftoff`, `raise`, `gaze`. **Bridge contract v2** (pass 2, additive): the state adds `v`, `runId` (moves on every load), `mode`, `caps[]`, `timeScale` (effective rate), `speedHold`, `speedHoldReason`, `waveElapsed`, `incoming[]` (the current wave's units still to spawn and the whole next wave, grouped by kind and route: `kind, count, route, air, armor, shield, heal, mini, boss, at, span, wave, eta`; `at`/`span` are game seconds on that wave's clock, `eta` is seconds from now or -1), `stats{kills, leaks, earned, streak, bestStreak, perfectWaves, waveLeaks[], leakKinds[], leakCounts[], dmgKinds[], dmgValues[]}` (`streak` counts consecutive leak-free waves and boss phases), `judgement{seq, kind, wave, value}` (flawless, clear, leak, phaseBreak, superForged, saved), `lossCauses[]` (top two leaking kind/route groups with `airUncovered` and a suggested `counter` tower), `carrotX`, `carrotY` and `spawnPoints[]` (viewport fractions, origin top-left); the boss adds `abilityTarget` (-1 when none), `abilityTargetTier`, `abilityTargets[]`, `remaining`, `eta`, `routeFraction`, `contactFatal`, `windup`, `actionable`, `thresholds[]`, `x`, `y`; minibosses add `castTotal`. New effects: `waveIncoming`, `judgement`, `bossDeadline`, `speedHold`, `timeScale`. The snapshot is written by hand (no NaN or Infinity can reach `JSON.parse`; 5.2 KB at 110 hurt enemies once the view sets `caps` "worldBars", which caps monster bars at 32). `game-data.json` levels gain `wavePlan`, `par` (the expert's median clear time x 1.1) and `modes`. `game-data.json` levels 25–27 carry `challenge` and `bossName`; towers carry `superName`, `superTrait` and five-entry `damage`/`interval`/`range`.

Campaign progression is local and single-player: victories unlock the next level; completion, an unharmed carrot and cleared scenery are separate medals. Replaying a level merges earned medals. Browser development preview is gated by `import.meta.env.DEV` and does not award campaign results. Simulation test helpers compile only under `UNITY_EDITOR`; production's JSON command switch has no free currency, forced damage, unlock-all or forced-win command. Client saves are not a competitive anti-cheat boundary.

## Verification boundary

The 177,365-assertion Unity run validates the 24-level campaign, the three challenges, ten powers and ten Supers, seven bosses, blindness, uproot and the wave gate: 54 policy-bot campaigns (two policies × 27 levels) won with earned coins, six merges-off control runs, poor single-position coverage lost on levels 1, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 25 and 27, and two deterministic replays (policy 0 on Scarecrow Field tick by tick for 22,757 ticks, and a scripted level-8 command list with a merge, an undo and a sale). See [balance-results.md](balance-results.md) and the retained [raw result log](simulation-results.txt).

These checks establish simulation behavior and legal economy. They do not certify rendering quality, phone frame rate, touch usability, music quality or every possible tower strategy. Runtime visual acceptance is a separate gate.
